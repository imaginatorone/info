# Voice and writing

The site should sound like a person made it for other people.

## Punctuation

- Use the normal hyphen "-" when a dash is needed.
- Do not use em dashes or en dashes in visible copy.
- Prefer short sentences, commas, periods, parentheses, and line breaks.
- Avoid decorative punctuation and excessive ellipses.

## Tone

- Direct, calm, specific, slightly informal.
- Short fragments are fine when they feel natural.
- Do not sound like a product launch, agency case study, startup landing page, or generated portfolio.
- Do not explain the aesthetic to the visitor. Let the site demonstrate it.
- Do not invent grand claims about the person behind the site.

## Avoid

- "passionate developer"
- "crafting digital experiences"
- "where creativity meets technology"
- "cutting-edge"
- "next-generation"
- "seamless"
- "innovative solutions"
- "unlock"
- "elevate"
- "reimagine"
- "journey"
- "welcome to my world"
- "digital playground"
- "pushing boundaries"
- "powered by"
- meta commentary about how the site was made

Do not use chatbot sparkles, glowing brains, neural-network diagrams, hexagon-tech motifs, fake holographic HUDs, or other generic machine-intelligence imagery.

## UI copy

Navigation should stay plain: about, work, code, sound, links.

Functional labels may be uppercase when there is a reason, for example PRESS.

Do not turn ordinary actions into lore. Prefer "sound", "mute", "open", "play", "back" over invented system terminology.

## Unknown copy

If personal copy is not known yet, keep a neutral placeholder in source code or leave the section intentionally sparse. Do not invent a biography to make a layout look complete.

## Source and repository style

Comments should explain non-obvious engineering decisions, not narrate obvious code.

Avoid unnecessary abstractions, ceremonial files, giant component factories, and template-style sections that exist only because portfolio sites usually have them.

Commit messages should describe the work normally and briefly.
